# 2026-10-20 — Unfinished Gold

## The weather

Bright. Buoyant. Unfinished.

A high, open gold: quick light moving through cool air, a warm centre, and a
few long trails drifting past the edge.

## What changed

- **Palette:** near-black blue air, a cool deep shadow, sea-green haze, warm
  parchment light, and amber-gold at the centre.
- **Light:** raised and widened into the upper right, brighter than the
  foundation, with a soft opening in the weather around it.
- **Sky:** thinner cloud cover and smoother movement, leaving a larger patch of
  cool open air below the light.
- **Streams:** more of them, quicker, with wide orbits and longer memories
  around the light. A handful are strays — slower, cooler, longer trails that
  leave the current and drift outward.
- **Grade:** higher exposure, gentle contrast, and slightly muted saturation so
  the gold glows rather than shouts.
- **Motion:** the sky is a little quicker; the streams carry the day's energy.

## Notes

The entry has a win at its centre: something pursued for a long time finally
arrived, and the day was allowed to feel good. A small imperfection in an
otherwise good meal was shrugged off. At the edge there is one quiet absence — a
reply that did not come — held lightly rather than mourned.

The page keeps the win warm and high in the frame. The absence is not a cloud
over it: it is the cool colour of the air and the few trails that drift away
from the light. No names, scores, or private details are recorded here.

## Addendum — the foundation

This page began the day as *Threshold*, a blank-page foundation. The first diary
entry arrived and replaced that mood with the day's weather; the foundation's
dark blue world and warm light remain the base of the same place.

## Addendum — the page answers back

Touch was added after the first entry: a drag now pulls a temporary current
through the clouds and streams, and a tap sends one slow ring through the air.
It is still the same day's weather — the disturbance borrows the existing
palette and light — and it settles back into the mood on its own. The amplitudes
live in this day's mood file, so another day can hold itself more tightly or
become more yielding.

## Addendum — drawing into water

The touch layer became water. A drag now leaves a wake: small drops along the
path spread into soft ripples, bend the clouds, and nudge the streams; a tap
drops a stone and the rings widen and fade over several seconds. The water
keeps no marks, only fading motion, which suits a day that was warm but not
yet finished.

## Addendum — two people in the room

The page can now share that water. A small relay pairs visitors into rooms of
two; when one person draws, the other sees the same ripples, and a link with
`?room=` lets two people meet in the same water. It is quiet by design: no
names, no visible controls, no stored drawings. Three visitors in one room are
turned away to draw alone. The relay and the page share one roof, so on the
published site the water is simply there; the plain static mirror stays solo.

## Addendum — Drawing Water

The page was renamed *Drawing Water* and given a new surface. The same sky
is now seen in dark water: wind wavelets shimmer the reflection, the bottom of
the frame looks down into the water while the top turns to a mirror, and the
gold light stretches and breaks into a narrow column of glints. The streams
lie down and glide across the surface.

- **Palette:** the air turned to water — a deeper blue-green body, a greener
  haze, wet-paper light, and the same gold, a little softer.
- **Light:** higher in the frame, so its glint path has room to run towards
  the viewer.
- **Sky:** slightly slower, with thinner rays; it is a reflection now.
- **Streams:** fewer, slower, longer, and flattened onto the surface.
- **Touch:** a stroke now draws a line of light into the water. The ink runs
  with the ripples, bleeds at its edges, and is gone within seconds; a tap
  lands like a bead of glass with a ring around it.

Nothing in the entry was private: the prompt was two words, taken both ways —
water drawn up from somewhere deep, and drawing into it.

## Addendum — the water is alive

The streaks of light read as shooting stars over a lake, and they were let go.
The water is inhabited instead: soft drifters, translucent bells with a light
inside, pulse through it on a slow heartbeat and trail long threads along the
paths they take. The water itself holds tiny living sparks that breathe and
flare wherever it is touched. Drawing into the water wakes everything near
the stroke, and the drifters nearby turn and come, unhurried, to see who is
there — then wander off again.

- **Streams:** off. **Life:** a couple of dozen drifters, slow pulse, long
  tendrils, loosely gathered around the light, very curious.
- **Water:** plankton sparks added.

The wish behind this change was for something that felt organic and personal,
not decoration: a place that answers when it is touched.

## Addendum — shy, and more company

The drifters became fewer and shyer: five at most, and any one that is
touched or brushed past flares, bolts away from the hand, and is gone within a
second, returning quietly somewhere else a little later. From a distance they
are still curious; up close they will not be caught. The rooms opened up too:
up to five people now share the same water, and strangers arriving without a
link are placed together rather than alone.

## Addendum — everyone their own colour

Each person in a shared room now draws in their own ink, picked from five
colours that belong to this evening: the gold of the light, seafoam, dusk rose,
lavender mist, and a moonlit blue. They are close in brightness so no one's
mark shouts over another's; the water simply shows that more than one hand is
in it.

## Addendum — false air

A second entry, later and lower: the day went off. Food that sat wrong, a
mood in the room that would not lift, and weather that pressed down without
ever breaking — air that looked like air but did not feel like it. Underneath
it, a plain question: whether the next day will be better.

**Three words:** close, stale, waiting.

- **Palette:** the blue-green went sour — olive-grey haze, a flat dark body of
  water, dulled paper light, and the gold burned down to an ember.
- **Light:** smaller, dimmer, nearly without rays, but kept in the same place.
- **Sky:** a low, heavy lid of cloud, a little more torn, barely moving. No
  rain: the weather never breaks, which is the point.
- **Water:** fewer glints, fewer sparks; the surface still answers a touch.
- **Life:** three drifters, tired and slow, warier than this morning. They
  stay close to the light and keep their distance from the hand.
- **Air:** more suspended dust and fewer soft orbs — air you can see.
- **Grade:** lower exposure and saturation, softer contrast.

The page does not answer the question with a sunrise. It keeps the ember and
the drifters alive under the lid: the same place, the same water, waiting for
the air to clear.

## Addendum — the water can be heard

The page has a voice now, and it is only the water's. After the first touch
a quiet bed fades in: water lapping somewhere out of sight, air moving over
it, and every so often a drip far away. A tap lands as a small plop with a
bubble rising in it; a stroke makes a hush, like a hand drawn through water.
Nothing is recorded; it is all made of noise, in the moment.

For tonight's close air the sound sits low: a muffled band of air, long tired
pauses between the swells, slightly heavier drops, and only a few drips.

## Addendum — more than one finger

The water now feels every finger, not just the first. Two or three fingers
drawn across it leave two or three lines of light side by side, each with its
own wake, its own drop where it lands, and its own hush.
